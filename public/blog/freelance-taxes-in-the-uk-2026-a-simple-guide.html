Freelance Taxes in the UK (2026)

September 25, 2026 8 min read Taxes

Freelancing in the UK comes with tax responsibilities, but navigating HMRC’s rules doesn’t have to be complicated. This guide breaks down everything you need to know about registering, deductions, deadlines, and tools to stay compliant.

Why Freelancers Must Register with HMRC

If you earn more than £1,000 from freelancing in a tax year (April 6 to April 5), you must register as self-employed with HMRC. This applies even if freelancing is a side hustle alongside a full-time job.

Failing to register can result in penalties, so it’s critical to act as soon as you start earning.

When and How to Register

Step 1: Register as Self-Employed

You can register online via the HMRC website. The process takes about 10 minutes, and you’ll need:

  • Your National Insurance number.
  • Personal details (name, address, date of birth).
  • The date you started freelancing.

After registering, HMRC will send you a Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) within 10 days (or 21 days if by post). Keep this safe—you’ll need it for your tax return.

Step 2: Set Up a Self-Assessment Account

Once registered, you’ll need to file a Self-Assessment tax return each year. This is where you report your income and expenses to calculate how much tax you owe.

Understanding Tax Deductions

One of the biggest advantages of freelancing is the ability to claim tax-deductible expenses. These reduce your taxable income, lowering the amount of tax you owe.

FAQs About Freelance Taxes in the UK

Do I need to pay VAT as a freelancer?

You only need to register for VAT if your taxable turnover exceeds the VAT threshold (£90,000 as of 2026). Most freelancers don’t need to worry about VAT unless they scale significantly.

What if I freelance part-time?

The same rules apply: if you earn over £1,000 in a tax year, you must register as self-employed and file a Self-Assessment. Your tax bill will depend on your total income (including employment income).

Can I claim for my home office?

Yes! You can claim a proportion of your rent, mortgage interest, utilities, and internet costs based on the percentage of your home used for work. For example, if your home office is 10% of your home’s total space, you can claim 10% of these costs.

What happens if I miss the deadline?

If you file your Self-Assessment late, you’ll automatically receive a £100 penalty, even if you don’t owe any tax. Further delays (3+ months) can result in additional fines of £10 per day, up to £900.
