Best Freelance Platforms UK (2026)

September 25, 2026 9 min read Freelancing

The best freelance platform for you depends on three things: your skill, your target client, and how much commission you're willing to give up. Here are the UK-relevant options ranked by category, with fee structures that most guides hide.

General marketplaces — the big four

Upwork

The largest general freelance marketplace, covering 130+ categories. Hourly, fixed-price, and packaged contracts.

  • Fees: 10% on contracts up to $1,000 total, 5% above that. Client pays a separate fee.
  • Payment protection: Strong. Hourly contracts have time-tracking with payment guarantee; fixed-price uses escrow.
  • Best for: Developers, writers, marketers, virtual assistants. Anyone targeting US clients.
  • Watch out for: "Connects" cost money to bid — around $0.15 each, and competitive projects cost 6–16 Connects.

Fiverr

Fixed-price "gigs" where you list a service and buyers purchase directly. No bidding.

  • Fees: 20% of every order. Flat.
  • Payment protection: Strong. Funds held until delivery.
  • Best for: Designers, video editors, voiceover artists, logo designers, quick-turnaround work.
  • Watch out for: 20% is the highest standard commission in the industry. Buyers see 5-star reviews more than portfolios.

Freelancer.com

Bidding + contests platform with 1,350+ categories.

  • Fees: 10% or $5 minimum per project.
  • Payment protection: Milestone-based escrow. Dispute process exists but slower than Upwork.
  • Best for: Developers, writers, designers, data entry.
  • Watch out for: More low-budget projects and lowball bids than Upwork. Quality varies widely.

PeoplePerHour

UK-founded, with strong UK client base.

  • Fees: Sliding rate — 20% on first £250 with a client, drops to 7.5% after £5,000 lifetime with that same client.
  • Payment protection: Escrow-based. Good.
  • Best for: UK-focused freelancers, especially in design, writing, marketing.
  • Watch out for: Smaller project volume than Upwork or Fiverr.

Premium / vetted platforms

Toptal

Top 3% of applicants. Vetted network for tech, finance, design, and consulting.

  • Fees: 0% to freelancers. Toptal bills clients directly and takes its cut from the client side.
  • Rates: Typically $60–$200+/hour.
  • Best for: Senior developers, consultants, designers with strong portfolios.
  • Watch out for: Difficult screening process (multiple interviews, live coding). Worth it if you pass.

Codeable

WordPress-only expert network. Strict vetting (~2% acceptance).

  • Fees: 17.5% to freelancer.
  • Best for: Experienced WordPress developers. Consistent flow of high-quality clients.

Braintrust

Talent-owned network. No freelancer fee — the network is owned by its users.

  • Fees: 0% to freelancers (10% to clients).
  • Best for: Tech, design, product roles targeting US companies.

UK-specific platforms

YunoJuno

UK-focused network for creative and tech contractors. Strong for freelance management, IR35 support.

  • Fees: 0% to freelancers (client pays).
  • Best for: UK-based designers, developers, project managers targeting agencies and brands.

Twine

UK creative marketplace — music, film, design, illustration.

  • Fees: Varies by project.
  • Best for: Creative freelancers who want UK-focused projects.

Where you should actually start

If you're a beginner:

  • Upwork — biggest volume of work, reasonable fees
  • Fiverr — good for building a portfolio fast, despite 20% fee
  • PeoplePerHour — if you're UK-based and want UK clients

If you're experienced:

  • Toptal — highest rates but hardest entry
  • Braintrust — 0% fee, US clients
  • YunoJuno — UK clients, no fee

The honest advice

Don't spread yourself across five platforms. Pick two: one general (Upwork or Fiverr), one premium (Toptal or Braintrust if you qualify). Focus 80% of your time on one.

The freelancers who make real money aren't the ones on the most platforms — they're the ones who built a strong profile on one, earned reviews, and got repeat clients.
