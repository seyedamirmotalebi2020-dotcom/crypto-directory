Prolific vs YouGov

September 21, 2026 5 min read Comparison

Prolific and YouGov are two of the most popular survey platforms in the UK — but they're built for completely different users. This is a direct comparison so you can pick the one that actually suits you.

Prolific — better pay, fewer studies

Prolific hosts research studies from universities and market research firms. Studies are longer (10–30 minutes typically), and pay £6–£15/hour on average — significantly higher than traditional panels.

Strengths

  • No minimum withdrawal. Withdraw any amount to PayPal instantly.
  • Higher pay per hour. A 20-minute study might pay £4–£5.
  • Low screen-out rate. Because studies are targeted, if you're invited you usually qualify.
  • Strong Trustpilot reputation. 4.5/5 from over 15,000 reviews.
  • Research-focused. Academic studies, so often on interesting topics.

Weaknesses

  • Study volume is inconsistent. Some users see 5+ studies a week; others see one a month. It depends heavily on your demographic.
  • No mobile app. Web only, though mobile web works fine.
  • Highly selective. Studies usually target specific demographics, so you can't just "do surveys whenever you want".

YouGov — higher volume, lower pay

YouGov is one of the UK's best-known research firms. As a panel member, you complete frequent surveys on politics, consumer habits, current events and lifestyle.

Strengths

  • High survey volume. Members typically get 3–10 survey invitations per week.
  • Well-known brand. Established reputation in the UK, especially for political polling.
  • Interesting topics. Surveys on current events and consumer trends.
  • Choice of reward. PayPal, vouchers, or charity donation.

Weaknesses

  • £50 minimum withdrawal. The single biggest drawback. At £3/hour average and 15 minutes/day, it takes 3–6 months to reach.
  • Modest pay per survey. Most surveys pay £0.50–£1.50.
  • Screen-out rate around 40%. Standard for a general panel.

Which one should you pick?

Choose Prolific if:

  • You want the highest pay per hour
  • You prefer fewer, higher-value studies
  • You want to withdraw immediately
  • You match a demographic that research often targets (students, professionals, specific age ranges)
  • You're willing to wait for study invites

Choose YouGov if:

  • You want steady survey volume
  • You don't mind the £50 threshold
  • You're interested in political and consumer surveys
  • You want a well-known UK brand
  • You're patient and can wait months for payout

Use both if:

  • You want the best of both worlds. There's no reason not to have both accounts. Prolific for higher-value studies; YouGov for filling dead time.

What about other options?

If neither Prolific nor YouGov is a good fit:

  • Toluna — high volume like YouGov, but with a lower £5 threshold
  • Ipsos i-Say — similar to YouGov, established global brand
  • Qmee — lowest threshold, fastest cashout
  • AttaPoll — mobile app, low threshold

The verdict

If you can only use one, start with Prolific. The pay rate is significantly higher, the threshold is zero, and the platform has a strong reputation. The only downside is that study availability depends on your demographics.

If Prolific doesn't offer you enough studies, add YouGov as a secondary platform. It pays less per hour, but you'll have something to do while waiting for Prolific invites.
