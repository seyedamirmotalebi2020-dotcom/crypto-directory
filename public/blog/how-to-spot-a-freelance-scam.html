How to Spot a Freelance Scam

September 25, 2026 7 min read Safety

Freelance scammers target new freelancers because they don't yet know the red flags. The patterns are consistent across every platform — Upwork, Fiverr, PeoplePerHour, email, LinkedIn. Here are the eight that should make you walk away.

Red flag #1 — The client wants to move off-platform immediately

Legitimate clients don't insist on WhatsApp or Telegram for the initial conversation. Scammers do, because they want the conversation outside the platform's protection system.

Rule: Do all initial communication inside the platform. Move to email only after you've been paid the first time.

Red flag #2 — The project pays unusually well for low skill

"£500 for a simple 5-minute data entry task" is a scam. So is "£100/hour for email checking, no experience needed".

Legitimate freelance work scales pay with skill and complexity. Any offer where the pay doesn't match the described effort is a setup.

Red flag #3 — They ask for money upfront

"Pay £50 for the starter kit", "send £30 to activate your account", "£20 registration fee to receive clients". None of these are legitimate. Freelancers are never required to pay clients or platforms to receive work.

The rule that never fails You should never pay money to earn money as a freelancer. Every legitimate platform (Upwork, Fiverr, PeoplePerHour) is free to receive work. They charge commission on income — never an upfront fee.

Red flag #4 — They ask for personal details early

Scammers want:

  • Your bank login (not just account number for payment)
  • Your National Insurance number
  • A copy of your passport or driving licence "for verification"
  • Your debit/credit card details
  • Your PayPal password

Legitimate clients only need:

  • Your name
  • Your email
  • Your bank account number and sort code (for payment only, at contract stage)
  • Sometimes a W-8BEN form for US clients (standard tax form)

Never share login credentials with anyone. No legitimate client needs them.

Red flag #5 — Overpayment and refund requests

The scam: "We sent you £500 by mistake — please refund £400 and keep £100 for the work." The original £500 was fraudulent (fake check, fake transfer) and will bounce. Your £400 refund is real and disappears with you.

This is the single most common freelance scam, running for over a decade. If a client "overpays" you, do not refund. Contact the platform immediately.

Red flag #6 — Unpaid "test projects"

A short, paid test project is legitimate. A two-week project described as a "test" is exploitation.

The rule: Anything over 1–2 hours of work should be paid. If the client wants to see quality first, offer a small sample (paid) or a link to your portfolio.

Red flag #7 — Vague scope with pressure to start

"We just need someone to handle the writing side, we'll figure out details as we go — can you start tomorrow?"

Legitimate projects have a clear deliverable, deadline, and scope. Vagueness usually means:

  • The client doesn't have a budget
  • The client doesn't know what they want
  • The scope will expand without additional pay

Red flag #8 — The client's profile is too good

A brand-new account with a 5-star rating, glowing reviews, and a large budget is suspicious. Scammers create fake profiles with fabricated reviews to appear legitimate.

What to check:

  • How long the client has been on the platform
  • Whether their payment method is verified
  • Total spent history, not just review count
  • Whether their reviews come from different freelancers

On Upwork and Fiverr, a "verified payment method" badge is a real signal. So is "payment verified" combined with a long history.

What to do if you've already been scammed

  1. Stop all communication. Do not send any more money.
  2. Report to the platform. Upwork, Fiverr, PeoplePerHour all have fraud teams.
  3. If you sent money, contact your bank immediately. Faster reporting = higher chance of recovery.
  4. If you gave login details, change passwords on every account. Enable 2FA.
  5. Report to Action Fraud (UK) at actionfraud.police.uk. Recovery is rare but reporting builds pattern evidence.
  6. Warn others — post on the platform's community forums and relevant subreddits.

The one-page checklist

Before accepting any freelance project, verify all of these:

  • ✅ Client has a verified payment method
  • ✅ Client has a history (not brand new account)
  • ✅ Project scope is clearly defined
  • ✅ Payment amount matches the effort
  • ✅ Communication stays on-platform initially
  • ✅ No upfront payment required from you
  • ✅ No personal documents requested before contract
  • ✅ Contract is through the platform, not a personal agreement

If any of these fail, walk away. There's always another client.
