Are Paid Surveys Worth It?

September 21, 2026 6 min read Paid Surveys

The honest answer is: it depends on your alternatives. If your alternative is watching TV, paid surveys pay better than nothing. If your alternative is an hour of freelance work, they don't. Here's the full breakdown.

The real numbers

Typical hourly rates on legitimate UK platforms in 2026:

  • Prolific — £6–£15/hour. Best case, but study availability varies by demographic. Some users only see 1–2 studies a week.
  • YouGov, Ipsos, Toluna — £3–£8/hour. Surveys pay £1–£3 each; most take 10–15 minutes.
  • Qmee, AttaPoll — £2–£5/hour. Lower per-survey pay but higher volume.
  • Swagbucks, ySense (surveys) — £2–£4/hour effective rate.

Now subtract screen-out time. If 40% of surveys screen you out mid-way, your effective hourly rate drops by 20–40%. So a "£6/hour" panel can easily become £4/hour.

The opportunity cost test

Ask yourself: what else could I be doing with this time?

  • If the answer is "nothing useful" — surveys are worth it. £4/hour is £4/hour.
  • If the answer is "paid work" — surveys are not worth it. Even minimum wage (£11.44/hour) beats the best survey rate.
  • If the answer is "learning a skill" — probably not worth it in the first six months, but could be in the long run.
  • If the answer is "another earning method" — depends. Freelancing pays more, but requires skills. Faucets pay less. Surveys sit in the middle.

How surveys compare to other methods

Approximate hourly rates across the main categories in our directory:

  • Freelancing — £10–£100+/hour. Highest pay, but requires marketable skills.
  • Content creation — variable. £0 for months, then £20+/hour once established.
  • Paid surveys — £3–£10/hour for a casual user.
  • App offers — £2–£8/hour, depending on how many milestone offers you complete.
  • Selling & reselling — £5–£30/hour effective, but requires items to sell.
  • CAPTCHA work — £0.50–£3/hour. Lowest rate but available worldwide, day one.
  • Play-to-earn games — £0.10–£2/hour. Fun, not lucrative.
  • Crypto faucets — £0.10–£1/hour. Lowest of all, but zero effort.

Surveys sit solidly in the middle: not the highest-paying, but the highest-paying that almost anyone can do without a specific skill or platform.

When surveys are worth it

  • You have dead time (commuting, waiting rooms, lunch breaks)
  • You want low-commitment side income
  • You don't have marketable skills you could be freelancing
  • You find the survey topics mildly interesting
  • You treat it as a hobby with a small paycheck, not income

When they're not

  • You need real income. £40/month won't pay rent.
  • You have skills (writing, coding, design) you could freelance instead
  • You find them boring — the hourly rate drops when you're not paying attention anyway
  • You're prone to chasing "big offers" that require purchases or subscriptions
  • You're looking for reliable, predictable earnings

How to get the most out of them

  1. Pick two platforms max. One high-pay (Prolific), one high-volume (YouGov or Toluna).
  2. Set a time limit. 15 minutes a day, or 30 minutes if you enjoy it. Don't let it become a job.
  3. Only do surveys above a certain rate. A £0.50 survey that takes 10 minutes is £3/hour — not worth it if higher-paying ones are available.
  4. Withdraw regularly. Don't leave £30 sitting on a panel for six months.
  5. Track your actual earnings. Every three months, tally what you've earned against the hours you've spent. If the rate is under £3/hour, stop.

My verdict

If you're already going to spend time on your phone, and you don't have a freelancing skill you could use instead, surveys are the highest-paying low-effort option available. £20–£80 a month is realistic for 15 minutes a day.

But be honest with yourself. Most people who sign up to survey sites quit within a month because they realise £5/hour isn't worth their attention. That's a legitimate conclusion. If it applies to you, surveys aren't the answer — go learn a skill or find a freelance niche instead.
