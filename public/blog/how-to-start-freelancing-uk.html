How to Start Freelancing in the UK

September 25, 2026 9 min read Freelancing

Most "how to freelance" guides start with "decide what you're good at" and end with "build your personal brand". They skip the part where you actually find a paying client. This guide covers the first 90 days — what to do, in what order, and what to avoid.

Step 1 — Pick a skill that already has demand

The easiest freelance skills to sell in 2026, ranked by how quickly they convert to first payment:

  1. Writing — blog posts, SEO content, product descriptions. Fastest to start, lowest barrier.
  2. Video editing — short-form (Reels, TikTok) is the highest demand category.
  3. Design — social posts, logos, presentations. Very fast turnaround work available.
  4. Virtual assistance — inbox management, scheduling, research.
  5. Web development — WordPress, Shopify, front-end. Higher rates, longer to first client.
  6. Copywriting — emails, ads, landing pages. Higher rates than general writing.

Pick one. Not two. Not "I'll do writing and design". Choose the one you can deliver at a competent level and commit to it for at least 90 days.

Step 2 — Build 2–3 portfolio samples

You don't need a client to have a portfolio. You need 2–3 pieces of work that demonstrate your skill:

  • Writers — write 2 blog posts on a topic you understand. Publish them on Medium or your own blog. Include real research and structure.
  • Designers — redesign 2 real brands' logos or landing pages. Publish on Dribbble or a personal site.
  • Video editors — edit 2 short-form videos from existing footage (YouTube clips, stock footage). Publish on your own social.
  • Developers — build 2 small projects and host them on GitHub. Include a live demo.

Aim for quality, not quantity. Three strong samples beat ten weak ones.

Step 3 — Set up the basics

  • Professional email — firstname@yourdomain.com or firstname.lastname@gmail.com
  • Simple portfolio — a Notion page, a Carrd site, or a personal domain. Not required to be fancy.
  • Profile photo — clear, professional, not a selfie. Fiverr and Upwork profiles with photos convert 3× better than without.
  • Payment method — PayPal, Wise, or a business bank account. Wise is cheaper for international clients.

Step 4 — Pick a starting platform

For beginners, the three fastest to first payment:

  • Upwork — biggest volume, but competitive. Apply to 5–10 jobs a day for the first two weeks.
  • Fiverr — list 3–5 gigs, share the links everywhere. First sale typically within 2–4 weeks.
  • PeoplePerHour — best for UK-based freelancers. Smaller volume but higher quality clients.

Choose one. Do not sign up for all three.

Step 5 — Price honestly

Realistic starting rates for UK freelancers in 2026:

  • Writer (blog / SEO) — £30–£80 per 1,000 words, or £15–£30/hour
  • Video editor (short-form) — £30–£80 per video
  • Designer (social posts) — £20–£60 per post
  • Virtual assistant — £10–£20/hour
  • Web developer — £25–£60/hour
  • Copywriter — £40–£120 per email or ad

Do not underprice to "get your first client". Low prices attract difficult clients and set a ceiling on what you can charge later. Start at the low end of the ranges above and raise as you build reviews.

Step 6 — The first 30 days

  1. Days 1–3 — Set up profile, portfolio, photo.
  2. Days 4–10 — Apply to 5–10 jobs per day. Personalize every proposal. Include a 1-line answer to the client's specific problem in the first sentence.
  3. Days 11–20 — Expect 1–3 responses. Most will ghost. First client typically converts here.
  4. Days 21–30 — Deliver the first project excellently. Ask for a 5-star review. Repeat.

Realistic first income: £50–£300 in month 1.

Common mistakes that kill new freelancers

  • Writing generic proposals. "Hi, I'm a writer and I'd love to work on your project" gets ignored. Specific proposals convert.
  • Undercharging. £5/hour attracts £5 clients.
  • Missing deadlines. Miss one, and your rating drops permanently.
  • Taking bad clients. The client who haggles on price is also the client who asks for 5 revisions.
  • Giving up in month 2. Most freelancers who quit do so between week 4 and week 8.
  • Not asking for reviews. A 5-star review after every project is worth more than another portfolio piece.
  • Skipping tax registration. Once you earn £1,000+ in a tax year, you need to register as self-employed with HMRC.

What to expect realistically
