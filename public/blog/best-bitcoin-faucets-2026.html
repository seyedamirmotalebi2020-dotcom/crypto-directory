Best Bitcoin Faucets of 2026 — Tested & Ranked

September 19, 2026 9 min read Reviews

The faucet landscape changes constantly — sites appear, pay for a while, degrade, and disappear. This list is based on data collected in September 2026, checking current payout evidence through FaucetPay, independent monitoring tools, site age, and community reviews. It's not a list of who promises the most. It's a list of who is actually paying right now.

How we tested

Every site here was checked against five signals:

  1. FaucetPay payout volume in the last 7 days — a faucet sending real money to real users is the strongest available signal
  2. Number of users paid in that window — a site paying £20 to 3 users is weaker than one paying £20 to 300
  3. Site age and continuity — has it survived multiple market cycles?
  4. Independent monitoring data — Faucet Monitor and similar tools track payment timestamps
  5. Community feedback — Trustpilot, Reddit and Trustpilot, weighted toward reviews from the last 90 days

We don't take payment for placement. Trust scores are editorial.

The shortlist

1. FaucetPay (★★★★★)

Not a faucet itself, but the essential foundation. FaucetPay is the micro-wallet that almost every site below pays through. If you're going to use more than two faucets, sign up here first — it makes everything else possible. Free to use, 20+ supported coins, instant internal transfers.

2. Freecash (★★★★★)

The highest-earning platform on this list by a wide margin, even though it's not technically a faucet — it's a GPT (Get-Paid-To) platform. Surveys, game offers, product trials and app installs pay $1–$10+ per hour depending on your country. First withdrawal requires ID verification. PayPal starts at $5 with a 5% fee. Best option if you want meaningful earnings rather than pennies.

3. Cointiply (★★★★☆)

One of the most established crypto earning sites, combining a faucet with surveys, offerwalls, PTC ads and games. Pays in BTC, DOGE, LTC and Dash to your own wallet. Withdrawal usually takes 24–72 hours. Minimums start at about $3 worth for DOGE/LTC/Dash, $5 for BTC. Recent user reports include occasional account holds and verification issues, so keep records.

4. Vie Faucet (★★★★☆)

One of the highest-volume faucets currently active. Recent FaucetPay data showed $595/week paid to 4,856 users across BTC, DOGE, ETH, LTC, TRX, USDT and others. Claim interval is 5 minutes, and the site combines faucet claims with PTC, shortlinks and challenges. Strong signal, though as with any faucet, test with a small withdrawal first.

5. Final Autoclaim (★★★★☆)

Auto-faucet that runs claims server-side — you don't need to keep a browser tab open. FaucetPay minimum is just $0.05, direct blockchain payouts start at $0.05–$5 depending on coin. FaucetPay health score is currently 100. Great option if you want passive earning in the background.

6. Coinzi (★★★★☆)

Multi-coin platform supporting PEPE, SHIB, BONK, FLOKI, BTC, TRX, LTC and more. Tasks, faucet, streaks and contests. Trustpilot currently shows about 4.9/5 from 254 reviews with recent 2026 activity. One of the cleaner newer listings in this space.

7. Knolix Money Tree (★★★★☆)

Long-running faucet listed on FaucetPay since March 2020. Currently pays $5.20/week to 31 users, rating 4.1, health 100. The faucet gradually fills over an hour, and you can claim when you choose — an unusual and pleasant mechanic compared to fixed-interval claims.

8. ARUBLE.NET (★★★☆☆)

Strong current volume — FaucetPay shows $188.51/week to 39 users, health 72, with 5-minute claim intervals across 20+ coins. Caution: reputation signals conflict. Trustpilot shows 4.6/5 from ~3,700 reviews, but an independent phishing analysis currently flags the domain. Test-only with disposable information.

Sites to avoid

Every one of these has been flagged for reasons that should make you walk away:

  • FreeBitco.in — announced shutdown and withdrawal freeze in late 2025. Do not sign up.
  • FREE-LTC-INFO — flagged by multiple security services for phishing and malware.
  • FCJ Faucet (faucet.cryptojobss.com) — ScamAdviser reports "Very Likely Unsafe".
  • TrustDice, Paradice, Pasino, Satoshi Hero — these are casinos, not faucets. Real-money gambling, 18+.
  • Express Wallet — listed on the Bank of Russia's official financial pyramid warning list.
Golden rule Faucets are a hobby, not income. Even the best sites pay tiny amounts per hour. If you're looking for meaningful extra money, GPT sites and offerwalls will pay 10–100× more. Faucets are for filling small pockets of time.

Frequently asked questions

Do Bitcoin faucets actually pay?

The legitimate ones do — but the amounts are tiny. A typical faucet pays £0.001–£0.01 per claim, and even at a 5-minute interval, that's pennies per hour. Anyone claiming faucets make a meaningful income is either mistaken or selling something.

What's the best Bitcoin faucet for beginners?

FaucetPay plus one or two sites from the shortlist above. Don't try to run twenty sites at once — you'll burn out and miss the ones that actually matter.

How much can I earn per month?

If you spend an hour a day across a handful of top faucets and GPT sites, expect £3–£15 per month. That's the honest number. Anyone promising more is misrepresenting how the economics work.

Are faucets legal in the UK?

Yes, using faucets is legal in the UK. Earnings may be taxable as income — see our article on crypto faucet legality and tax for details.

Which faucet has the lowest minimum withdrawal?

On our current data, Final Autoclaim (via FaucetPay) has one of the lowest at just $0.05. BitClickz advertises $0.01 minimum with no FaucetPay fee. Check the live directory for current values.
