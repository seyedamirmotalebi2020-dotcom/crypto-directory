Methodology

How We Rate Every Site

A trust score is not a marketing number. Here is exactly how it is calculated, what signals go into it, and what it does — and does not — guarantee.

Signals we use

Each site's editorial score weighs several factors. None is definitive on its own; the pattern across them is what matters.

  • Live payout evidence. Has the site sent any payment through FaucetPay in the last 7 days? How many users were paid? How much in total? A site paying $20 to 300 users is a stronger signal than one paying $20 to 3 users.
  • FaucetPay health score. FaucetPay computes a health metric for each listed faucet. We use it as a signal, not the signal — some low-health sites still pay, and some high-health sites are tiny.
  • Site age. A site running continuously for 4+ years has survived multiple market cycles and policy changes. A site registered last month has not.
  • Community votes. Anonymous, one per device per site, blended with the editorial score once a site has at least three votes.
  • Published rules and clarity. Sites that clearly state minimum withdrawals, prohibited practices, and rules score higher than sites with vague FAQs.
  • Known complaints. Patterns of "withdrawal pending for weeks" across independent review sites are penalised hard.

What the score does not mean

A high score does not mean "you are guaranteed to get paid". It means: as of the last check, this site shows evidence of currently paying, and no pattern of complaints suggests otherwise. Sites can fail at any time. Always do a small test withdrawal before investing significant time.

Safety Guide

How to Stay Safe in Crypto Earning

The "make money online" space is full of scams that specifically target people looking for free crypto. Follow this guide every time you sign up somewhere new.

Always Do

  • Use a dedicated email address for signups — never your primary one.
  • Use a burner wallet with tiny balances for faucet earnings. Never your main wallet.
  • Withdraw as soon as you hit each site's minimum. Never leave balances sitting.
  • Check the URL character by character before entering credentials.
  • Enable 2FA where offered — on FaucetPay especially.
  • Research the site independently before signing up. Look for recent Trustpilot, Reddit and Bitcointalk threads.
  • Do a small test withdrawal before trusting a site with hours of your time.
  • Report scam sites and vote "Scam" here so others are protected.

Never Do

  • Never share a wallet seed phrase or private key — with anyone, ever.
  • Never enter your seed phrase into a website. No legitimate faucet needs it.
  • Never pay an "activation fee", "verification fee" or "unlock fee".
  • Never invest money in a site promising a fixed daily return.
  • Never install browser extensions or APKs from a faucet's ad links.
  • Never use a VPN where the site's rules prohibit it — your balance will be voided.
  • Never trust a site solely because a YouTuber promotes it. Many are paid placements.
  • Never click ad links offering "multiply your crypto" or "3% daily returns". These are Ponzi schemes.

Red flags to walk away from immediately

  • "Invest $10, get $1 back every day." Textbook Ponzi scheme. There is no real business paying that — the money comes from new investors until it doesn't.
  • Withdrawal blocked until you invite 5 friends. Recruitment-based income is a hallmark of pyramid structures.
  • "Send a small verification payment to unlock your balance." No legitimate site needs you to send money to withdraw money.
  • Site asks for your wallet's seed phrase. This is the single clearest sign of a phishing attack. Close the tab.
  • Guaranteed profits, no risk. Nothing in crypto is risk-free. The word "guaranteed" attached to a profit is a warning, not a promise.
  • WhatsApp / Telegram "signals" groups offering to trade your crypto for you. These are almost universally scams.
  • Unsolicited DMs from "support" offering to recover your account. Legitimate support never DMs first.

What to do if you think you have been scammed

  1. Stop sending anything immediately. Do not send more money to "release" funds. That is how the loss compounds.
  2. Change passwords on every account that shares the email or password you used.
  3. If a wallet seed was exposed, move funds immediately to a new wallet with a fresh seed. Assume the compromised one is burned.
  4. Report the site to us via the vote button so it is flagged for other users.
  5. Report to your local cybercrime authority (Action Fraud in the UK, IC3 in the US, ACSC ReportCyber in Australia). Recovery is rare but reporting builds the pattern that gets these sites shut down.

A word about "guaranteed daily profit" schemes

These have proliferated since 2020 and are almost without exception Ponzi schemes. The structure is always the same: a slick landing page, testimonials (often fake), a payment gateway that looks professional, and a promise of "2% daily" or "15% weekly". Early investors sometimes do get paid — that is the trap, because their testimonials bring in the next wave of money, which funds the payments until the operator disappears with everything. If a site promises any fixed daily return, it is a Ponzi scheme. There are no exceptions in practice.

Everything You Need to Know

Comprehensive answers to the questions users ask most — from how much you can realistically earn to how to avoid the scams that plague this space.

How much money can I realistically earn from crypto faucets?

Realistically, faucets pay very little per claim — often fractions of a cent. A dedicated user working a handful of sites for an hour a day might earn a few dollars a month. Faucets are best treated as a hobby, not income.

GPT sites, offerwalls and surveys pay considerably more per hour — often $1–$10 per hour depending on your country and the offers available. If you are looking for real extra cash, spend most of your time there, and use faucets to fill gaps.

What is FaucetPay and why do most faucets use it?

FaucetPay is a free micro-wallet designed for small crypto payments. Instead of each faucet sending a separate on-chain transaction (which costs network fees), faucets send your balance to your FaucetPay account, and FaucetPay aggregates everything. Internal FaucetPay-to-FaucetPay transfers are free and instant.

This makes cents-level payments practical where they otherwise wouldn't be. If you plan to use more than two faucets, a FaucetPay account is essential.

How does the trust score work?

Each site has a trust score between 0 and 100. The score combines editorial review (site age, live payment evidence, FaucetPay health, known complaints) with anonymous community votes cast by visitors. Sites need at least three votes before the community score takes effect, and the display blends both so a single brigade cannot game it.

What do the status labels mean (Active, Under Review, Paused, Defunct, Blacklisted)?
  • Active — currently paying with recent verifiable evidence.
  • Under Review — listed and possibly paying, but signals are weak or mixed.
  • Paused — technically alive but with such low volume it is not worth your time.
  • Defunct — the site no longer functions or has shut down.
  • Blacklisted — not a free-crypto-earning site, usually a casino or gambling platform, or a service flagged for scams.
Can I trust the sites listed here?

No directory can guarantee any third-party site. Our listings show what we can currently verify — live payout activity, minimum withdrawals, coin support — but crypto earning sites can change owners, get acquired, or turn bad at any time.

Always do your own due diligence, never invest money, never share a seed phrase or private key, and do a small test withdrawal before trusting a site with more of your time.

Report it and leave.

What is a Ponzi scheme and how do I spot one?

A Ponzi scheme pays earlier investors with money from later investors, without any real underlying business. Red flags:

  • Guaranteed daily returns (e.g. "2% daily forever").
  • Fixed monthly percentages.
  • "Invest $X get $Y daily" style promises.
  • Recruitment-based income — you have to invite others to unlock withdrawals.
  • Withdrawal delays that "require" you to bring in more users.
  • Unclear or non-existent revenue source.

Genuine faucets never promise returns — they pay you small amounts for watching ads or completing tasks. Anything promising to multiply your money is almost certainly a Ponzi scheme.

Can I use a VPN with these sites?

Most faucets explicitly prohibit VPNs and proxies in their terms of service. If you use one, expect your account and balance to be banned without notice. Some sites (Freecash, Idle-Empire) are more lenient but still restrict certain regions.

Check each site's rules before signing up. The directory notes VPN restrictions where the site states them.

How often can I claim from a faucet?

Claim intervals vary widely — from 30 seconds (TFaucet) to 60 minutes (CoinDrip) to hourly (easygoingfaucet's ETH faucet). Each card in the directory shows the interval in the "Claims" line so you can see at a glance how frequently you can collect.

Shorter intervals usually mean smaller rewards per claim. Longer intervals usually mean a slightly larger claim but slower accumulation.

What is the difference between a faucet, PTC, offerwall and GPT site?
  • Faucets pay small amounts of crypto for a simple click, usually on a timer.
  • PTC (Paid-To-Click) sites pay you to view ads for a fixed duration.
  • Offerwalls are aggregated third-party tasks — install an app, complete a survey, try a game — that pay larger rewards.
  • GPT (Get-Paid-To) sites combine all three plus surveys, videos and tasks into one platform. GPT sites generally pay the most per hour.
Which sites pay the most per hour?

In general, from highest to lowest hourly earnings:

  1. GPT sites and offerwalls — often $1–$10+/hour depending on your country and the offers available.
  2. Surveys — typically $1–$5/hour.
  3. PTC ads — usually $0.10–$1/hour.
  4. Faucets — the lowest-earning category, but also the lowest-effort.

The best strategy for most users is one or two GPT sites plus FaucetPay for micro-faucets.

What is FaucetPay health score?

FaucetPay assigns each listed faucet a health score between 0 and 100 based on how reliably the faucet processes its FaucetPay payouts. High health scores are a positive signal, but they are not a guarantee — some sites have high health and low volume, others have low health but pay consistently.

We treat health as one signal among several, not the whole picture.

Can I earn without giving my personal details?

Most faucets require only an email address or a username and a FaucetPay wallet address. Some GPT sites (Freecash, Idle-Empire, PrizeRebel) require identity verification (KYC) before your first withdrawal — a photo ID and sometimes a selfie.

If a site asks for your seed phrase, private key, or bank login, it is a scam. Never provide those.

What is the minimum withdrawal on most sites?

FaucetPay-supported sites usually allow withdrawals from as little as $0.0001 to $0.20. Direct wallet withdrawals typically start at $1–$5 because of network fees. GPT sites usually start at $5 (PayPal) or $1 (crypto).

Each directory listing shows the minimum withdrawal in the offer line so you can sort by lowest threshold.

Is this legal? Are crypto faucets legal in my country?

In most countries, earning small amounts of crypto from faucets is legal — you are effectively being paid in crypto for watching ads. Tax treatment varies: in many jurisdictions these earnings count as income and should be declared. Check your local tax rules.

Some gambling-adjacent sites and crypto casinos are restricted in certain countries (for example, the UK requires a Gambling Commission licence for remote gambling). Always check before signing up.

What should I do if a site stops paying?

Stop claiming, do not invest more time, and vote "Scam" or "Unsure" on the site from the directory so other users are warned. If you had a balance stuck, some faucet monitoring communities track withdrawal failures publicly.

Never pay a "release fee" or "verification fee" to unlock a stuck balance — that is a scam on top of the original problem.

How do I avoid crypto scams?
  • Never pay an upfront "activation" fee.
  • Never share your wallet's seed phrase or private key with anyone.
